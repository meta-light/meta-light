# Storytelling

Source: https://x.com/shotguncaio/status/2003867438685749259

Storytelling is a skill, not a talent, and it improves with practice, not theory: the author got better by writing every day. A story sticks when it is **clear, human and intentional**. It doesn't need to be dramatic.

## The shape of a short story

Most stories that stick follow this order. The lessons below are the tools for each part.

| Part | Job | Lessons |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Make them read the second sentence | 1 Start with action, 7 Hook fast |
| Tension | Make them want the outcome | 6 Create tension |
| Middle | Make it real and felt | 3 Add emotion, 5 Use vivid details, 11 Make it relatable |
| Turn | Show what changed | 2 Use contrast |
| Ending | Leave them thinking, or acting | 10 End with impact, 13 Stories sell |

Lessons 4, 8, 9 and 12 (simple words, rhythm, no fluff, talking to the reader) apply to every sentence.

## The 13 lessons

| # | Lesson | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Start with action.** Skip the buildup; start where others would end. | "I woke up early and made coffee…" | "The brakes failed. The truck wasn't stopping." |
| 2 | **Use contrast.** Show the before and the after. | "I used to be bad at sales." | "I went from broke and ignored to closing $10K deals in 30 days." |
| 3 | **Add emotion.** If they don't feel it, they won't remember it. | "I launched my business in 2020." | "My hands shook as I hit 'publish', terrified it would flop." |
| 4 | **Keep it simple.** Short words, short sentences. | "I embarked on a transformative entrepreneurial journey." | "I started a business. It changed my life." |
| 5 | **Use vivid details.** Show, don't tell. | "I was nervous." | "My stomach twisted. My palms were drenched." |
| 6 | **Create tension.** Make them wait for the outcome. | "I got the job." | "I walked into the interview, heart pounding. No idea if I'd leave with an offer." |
| 7 | **Hook fast.** The first sentence decides whether they read on. | "Let me tell you a story about success." | "I lost everything in 48 hours." |
| 8 | **Use rhythm.** Read it aloud; if it sounds bad, rewrite it. | One long sentence that winds on and doesn't flow. | Short. Punchy. Flows. |
| 9 | **Cut the fluff.** Every word earns its place. Clarity beats cleverness. | "In order to succeed, one must be diligent." | "Success requires discipline." |
| 10 | **End with impact.** The ending matters more than you think. | "That's my story." | "That mistake cost me $50K. I never made it again." |
| 11 | **Make it relatable.** Use real moments people recognize, not abstractions. | "Failure is part of success." | "I bombed my first sales call. Stuttered through every word. Now I close deals in my sleep." |
| 12 | **Speak directly.** Talk to the reader, like a conversation, not a lecture. | "Many entrepreneurs struggle with pricing." | "You're undercharging. You know it. But you're scared to raise prices." |
| 13 | **Stories sell.** People buy the transformation, not the information. Sell the result, not the process. | "Our program includes 10 lessons and weekly calls." | "You'll go from invisible online to landing inbound clients every week." |

## Workflow

### Writing a story

1. **Find the moment.** Ask: what is the single scene where things were most at stake? The story starts there (lesson 1), not at the background.
2. **Collect the real material** before drafting: what happened, what the user saw and felt physically, the numbers, the before and after, what they learned. If the user hasn't given these, **ask** for them. Don't invent them (see Guardrails).
3. **Write the hook** as one sentence of action or stakes. Draft three and pick the strongest.
4. **Draft in the order of the table above:** hook, tension, middle, turn, ending. Hold back the outcome until the turn.
5. **Write the ending last.** Pick one: the cost or lesson ("I never made it again"), the change in the reader's terms, or a call to act. Never a summary like "That's my story."
6. **Edit** with the checklist below.

### Critiquing or rewriting a draft

Go through the checklist. For each failure, quote the sentence, name the lesson, and give the rewrite. Fix the hook and the ending first: they matter most.

## Editing checklist

- [ ] The first sentence is action or stakes, not setup, context or "let me tell you" (1, 7)
- [ ] There's a clear before and after (2)
- [ ] The outcome is held back until it's earned (6)
- [ ] At least one feeling is shown through a physical detail rather than named (3, 5)
- [ ] There is a concrete moment the reader has lived, not a moral stated in the abstract (11)
- [ ] No long words where short ones work; no sentence the reader has to reread (4, 9)
- [ ] It sounds right read aloud (8)
- [ ] It speaks to "you" where it gives advice or sells (12)
- [ ] Where it sells, it sells the result, not the features (13)
- [ ] The last line lands: a cost, a lesson or a change, not a sign-off (10)

## Guardrails

- **Use only true details.** The examples above ($10K deals, a $50K mistake, shaking hands) are illustrations. In the user's story, use their real numbers, feelings and events. Where a detail would help and is missing, ask for it or mark it `[detail?]`. Never make up a figure, a result or an emotion, least of all in sales copy, where invented results are misleading.
- **Don't overdo it.** Fragments, one-line paragraphs and arrows are tools, not a style. A whole post of three-word lines reads as a template. Mix them with normal sentences (and see **stop-slop**, which flags dramatic fragmentation and punchy one-line endings when they become a habit).
- **Match the venue.** A thread or post can take the full treatment. An intro email, a research note or a work update needs one concrete moment and a clear point, not suspense. For arguments and essays, use **good-writing**; for research, **research-writing**; for X formats, **x-posts**.
- **Clarity first.** If a dramatic version is harder to follow than the plain one, keep the plain one.

## Output

- **Writing:** the story, then two or three alternative hooks and endings to choose from, then any `[detail?]` gaps the user should fill.
- **Critique:** the failed checklist items, each with the quoted line, the lesson, and the rewrite; then the revised story if the user asked for one.
