# Newsletters

How to write a research newsletter issue and the smaller pieces that go with it. Voice and formatting come from `style.md`. Aim for a draft that needs no further editing.

## Issue structure

Default layout for a daily or weekly research newsletter. Adjust sections to the publication, but keep the targets roughly proportional.

| Section | Target | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intro | 50 to 80 words | Two or three sentences, one hard fact. |
| Market update | 250 to 350 words | No heading. Ends with a byline. |
| Sponsor block (optional) | as supplied | Set off with `---` dividers above and below. |
| Feature | 400 to 600 words | Heading is the topic title. Byline. |
| Listen & Read | 300 to 400 words | Three items of 100 to 120 words each. |
| Trending | two to six posts, four is typical | Minimal bridging commentary. |
| Total | ~1,200 to 1,400 words | |

When two authors share an issue, one usually writes the intro and market update and the other writes the feature. Bylines link to each author's X profile.

### Intro

Two or three sentences that set up the issue with one specific fact. No "Welcome back", no "In this issue", no questions.

### Market update

Lead with an insight, not a number. Focus on what changed since the last issue and don't repeat its points.

1. P1: BTC against benchmarks (equities, gold, rates), with a chart.
2. P2: macro, only if something notable happened.
3. P3: cross-sector moves across crypto, with a chart.
4. P4 to P5: drill into the sector that moved most, ending with a one or two sentence outlook.

### Feature

One topic, argued. Thesis in the first paragraph, then the data, then a concrete implication for the reader. Apply `argumentation.md` at a smaller scale: one mispricing or tension, evidence, what to watch.

### Listen & Read

Three items that share one or two themes. Formula:

> [Source] released a report on [topic]. The report discussed [key finding with a number]. [Why it matters]. [Read more](URL)

### Trending

Pick posts from the last 24 to 48 hours with real signal from credible authors. Add a sentence of bridging context only where the post needs it.

## Standalone formats

### Podcast takeaways

- Bullets formatted as `**Topic header:** one to three sentences.`
- Past tense: "We discussed...", "[Guest] argued...".
- Every bullet carries a specific number or claim.
- Under 300 words total.

### Document summaries

- Start with "[Org] released/published [document type] on [topic]...".
- 50 to 100 words each.
- Self-contained: a reader who sees only this summary gets the point.

### Subject line and preview text

- Subject line: five words or fewer, specific to the lead story.
- Preview text: 40 to 60 characters, at most one emoji, adds information the subject line doesn't.

## Checks before sending

**Coherence**

- [ ] Every planned section is present and in order.
- [ ] Word counts are within range.
- [ ] Links resolve and anchor text is descriptive.
- [ ] Bylines are correct.
- [ ] The sponsor block is clearly separated from editorial content.
- [ ] Market data is current as of the send date.
- [ ] Numbers and terminology are consistent across sections.

**Density pass**

- [ ] Cut throat clearing ("In this issue", "Let's take a look", "It's been a busy week").
- [ ] Hedges kept only where the uncertainty is real.
- [ ] Filler words swapped out (see the avoid list in `style.md`).
- [ ] Sentences under 30 words on average; anything over 40 rewritten.
- [ ] Paragraphs of two to four sentences; anything over six split.
- [ ] Read aloud once.

Then run the final pass checklist in `style.md` and the stop-slop skill.
