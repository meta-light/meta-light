# **Venture Investment Framework**

## **Investment Philosophy**

### **Think in probabilities, not predictions**

> * **Investing is a [probabilistic-outcomes](https://cobie.substack.com/p/probabilistic-thinking) business:** Map the plausible future scenarios, assign each a probability and a payoff, and act only when the weighted risk/reward is favorable.   
> * **Ground scenario probabilities in history:** past drawdowns, prior bull/bear cycles, and crowd behavior in related markets. Journal the reasoning so it can be reviewed later.   
> * **Monte Carlo simulation formalizes this:** sample uncertain inputs repeatedly from probability distributions and aggregate the outcomes. In finance it is used to value projects, model schedules, price options and estimate default risk. Useful for modeling portfolio/fund outcome distributions.

### **Fundamentals over flows**

> * **Two opposing [approaches](https://dba.xyz/fundamentals-vs-flows/):** fundamental investing (quantifiable cash yield or utility) vs. flows investing (relying on a future buyer at a higher price). Confidence in a prediction falls the more it depends on human psychology.  
> * **Acid test:** would you hold this asset if you could never sell it?  
> * **Crypto fundamental investing leans toward growth rather than value:** a loss-making asset with strong future earnings can beat a shrinking profitable one. Flow-driven assets (memes, DAT trades) lose their floor when the trend stops.

### **Valuation matters**

> * **Revenue growth does not guarantee returns if you [overpay](https://x.com/santiagoroel/status/1996545911263309963):** Snowflake shares fell over five years while revenue grew several-fold, and the same dynamic likely applies to most L1s.  
> * [**Generalized L1s**](https://obviously.substack.com/p/the-exponent-is-real-but-so-is-gravity) **trade at extreme price-to-sales multiples** relative to mature businesses of similar size; app-layer winners show far more grounded multiples. The race between L1s is ultimately a race to build the global exchange.  
> * Systems that absorb capital faster than they build "organs" (revenue, retention, pricing power, recurring demand) tend to collapse. Re-underwrite constantly; pair optimism with skepticism.   
> * **Adoption [headlines](https://obviously.substack.com/p/why-your-coin-isnt-pumping) do not lift prices that are already priced to perfection:** Staking rewards are emissions/dilution, not earnings; real economic value is user-paid fees, tips and MEV. Onchain revenue is "re-occurring" (cyclical, speculative) rather than recurring SaaS revenue and deserves lower multiples.  
> * **Traditional [P/E](https://defieducation.substack.com/p/why-pe-ratios-are-broken-in-crypto) breaks in crypto:** don't annualize a hot 30-90 days; model forward scenarios mixing high, moderate and low activity. Evaluate token emissions separately (or use P/S).

### **Non-consensus and right**

> * **Nearly all venture returns come from [non-consensus-right deals](https://x.com/hosseeb/status/2026747999939080217):** consensus deals get bid up and cap upside. Hit rate matters less than being in the one or two deals that define a vintage, so ask of every deal: can this return the fund? The top three investments typically out-return the rest of the fund combined.  
> * **Resist trend-following:** chasing the hot theme yields a portfolio of whatever was popular a year or two earlier. Invest in what will matter in 3-5 years.  
> * **True [contrarianism](https://x.com/hosseeb/status/2008562546853261497) means people think you are wrong**, not that they retweet your take. Startups win through "preferential attachment": once resources (talent, capital, customers, brand) start attaching to them, it compounds. A top-tier lead investor [acts](https://x.com/david__booth/status/1981377270792253847) as a credibility bridge, and the Series A/B lead is a strong leading indicator of outcome.

### **Quality of thinking**

> * [**Poor thinking**](https://x.com/thedankoe/status/2016200242690195509) **is one-dimensional, reductionist, tribal and stops questioning too early:** Distinguish "knowing" (horizontal domain facts) from "understanding" (vertical sophistication of how you reason); hold threatening ideas as possibilities instead of absolutes.  
> * **Confidence curve**: trust in your own view rises fast with a little knowledge (the "mount of stupidity"), drops as you learn how much you don't know ("valley of despair"), then climbs steadily in line with real knowledge. Before taking high conviction, check which part of the curve you're on.

## **Evaluation Framework**

### **Team & Execution**

> * **Shipping velocity**: how often they deploy, whether they iterate on feedback, and whether the work is open source. Architecture should come before feature count.  
> * **Signal quality**: look for product fit that is improving, not just activity. Shipping the wrong thing is worse than shipping nothing.  
> * **Founder quality**: track record, domain expertise and integrity. Judge ideas on their merits rather than on who proposes them or how old they are; most people, experts included, are confidently wrong.  
> * **Adaptability**: prefer founders who act and correct mistakes over ones paralyzed by perfection, and who change course when the evidence turns rather than defend a failing bet.  
> * **Direction is the job**: the founder's core responsibility is deciding what the company works on.  
> * [**Founder archetypes**](https://x.com/cryptovlat/status/1981679847005905324) **across the adoption curve**: during the installation phase the leading founders are Tinkerers (can we combine smart contracts with blockchains?) and Evangelists (DeFi is changing the world). In the deployment phase it shifts to Integrators, who take built-out infrastructure and acquire distribution, and later Stewards, who protect and optimize. Match the archetype to where the sector is; crypto is now moving into the integrator phase. Watch for adverse selection: the type of founder a hot sector attracts is not always the type it needs. That phase [rewards](https://www.decentralised.co/p/non-consensus-and-right) experienced, pragmatic outsiders solving concrete infrastructure problems over insiders selling narratives.  
> * **Articulateness is not intelligence**: the relationship is a horseshoe. Polish peaks around normal intelligence, while both very smart and very dumb people can be inarticulate. Don't let a polished pitch stand in for depth, and don't dismiss a technical founder who presents poorly.  
> * **Founder traits for infrastructure/asset networks**: deep [grasp](https://www.lightshift.xyz/blog/asset-operating-system) of the asset's legal, technical and market dynamics, and the ability to aggregate a minimum viable set of critical supply early enough to force incumbents to integrate.   
> * [**Diligence**](https://x.com/TheiaResearch/status/1990450548685033958) **as an ongoing process**: you learn vastly more about a team after a few months of watching them build. Structures that allow investors to keep updating (and to unwind a treasury if the idea fails) let teams raise faster.  
> * **Discount [farmed](https://x.com/hosseeb/status/1988383380963291519) metrics**: airdrop farming is paid, simulated usage; heavily discount any easily-farmed metric and dig into the data. Good projects get farmed, but farming does not make a project good (correlation, not causation). [Airdrops](https://x.com/a_vaunt) in general have been a poor way to acquire users.  
> * **Founder [operating principles](https://x.com/0FJAKE/status/2008283160157602124) worth screening for**: market cap should lag product, not lead it; buy back strategically rather than automatically; don't call things "inevitable" or tell people to buy the token; default to public, sourced communication; avoid recurring commitments and maintenance load; keep the team small. 

### **Fundamentals Filter**

> * **The filter**: demand fundamentals first. Early, high-growth companies are fine, but there must be a credible thesis for real cash flows. An asset with no revenue potential that relies entirely on new buyers is speculation, not investment. Teams shouldn't launch a token before they have sustainable revenue, since long-run price follows revenue or a credible path to it ([avaunt.xrd](https://x.com/a_vaunt)).  
> * **Revenue model**: how does it make money today, not how could it? Separate contracted ARR from a run rate annualized off one strong month.  
> * **Unit economics**: margin structure and a path to profitability.  
> * **Sustainability test**: does it keep working without new capital coming in? Check [runway](https://defieducation.substack.com/p/why-pe-ratios-are-broken-in-crypto): treasury cash divided by monthly burn.  
> * [**Where crypto revenue comes from**](https://x.com/a_vaunt): financial engineering is what crypto does best, and perp DEXs, prediction markets, stablecoins and DeFi are the businesses that consistently make money. The fastest companies to \$100M in revenue are trading businesses. Infrastructure earns mainly from fees on contested state (trading), while plain transfers are a loss leader, so stablecoin chains need yield on the float or subscriptions to make money.  
> * **Red flag language**: guarantees ("guaranteed," "sure thing," "no-brainer," "no downside," "we can cap your downside"), urgency ("need to hurry," "opportunity of a lifetime," "you can't afford not to own it"), insider appeals ("the smart money is buying it," "exclusive," "trust me," "no one else knows how to do this") and unverifiable edge ("proprietary model," "can beat the market," "offshore strategy").  
> * **Token vs. equity**: tokens historically gave cap tables quick liquidity without a [legal claim](https://www.decentralised.co/p/non-consensus-and-right) on business value. Winners going forward tie revenue back to the token or use tokenized equity with legal recourse.  
> * **Not everything needs a token**: [not every product](https://assets.ctfassets.net/qtbqvna1l0yq/4hoFcrKkUm9Z5HiEX16g9J/e8a12f87497476841ff0d3defba944b8/Multicoin_Capital%C3%A2__s_Investment_Thesis.pdf) belongs on a permissionless chain and not every token can capture sustainable value. Well-designed tokens are powerful for programmable ownership and customer acquisition.  
> * [**Narrative vs. revenue**](https://x.com/embrron/status/2002094880244531449): narrative gets users in the door, revenue keeps them; today both have to work.  
> * **Post-launch token performance as a base rate**: in a [tracker](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1PF7rDVcIzUdlJF5eMdP4RQLl5dGxwHAnY4WdeV2reNE/edit?gid=413775341#gid=413775341) of 2025 token launches, the bulk of listed tokens are down roughly 60-94% in FDV from launch (e.g. Berachain, Plasma, Linea, Walrus). Treat launch FDV as a ceiling, not a floor.   
> * **Plain-language [test](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DPuF9axAY5w/)**: one heuristic for spotting BS is the ratio of Latin-derived to Germanic words. Germanic words are about a quarter of English vocabulary but most of everyday speech; Latin-derived words entered through Norman French and Renaissance scholarship and cluster in law, bureaucracy and rhetoric. A pitch heavy on abstract Latinate jargon and light on plain words deserves more scrutiny. 

### **Structural Advantages**

> * **Cost or product advantage**: either matches the incumbent's features at lower cost, or delivers better UX, speed or capability. A cost advantage often funds a product advantage later.  
> * **Durability**: will the advantage persist or erode quickly? Look for network effects that strengthen with scale and retention that shows customers stay.  
> * **"[Does the customer still pay when AI can check the work?](https://obviously.substack.com/p/does-the-customer-still-pay-when)"** If a business's pricing relies on friction or opacity, AI lets customers verify or do the work themselves and margins compress. Prefer businesses aided by technology over pure software (which keeps getting cheaper to copy), and trust-heavy services that price on outcomes rather than seats, tokens or hours.   
> * [**Inverse moats**](https://obviously.substack.com/p/the-exponent-is-real-but-so-is-gravity): open-source, commoditized L1s with zero switching costs win adoption but lose value capture to applications and to incumbents that own users (Stripe, JPMorgan, BlackRock).   
> * [**Shared supply as a moat**](https://www.lightshift.xyz/blog/asset-operating-system): asset-centric networks (Assets → Apps → Users) become the coordination layer many apps plug into, compounding via network effects. Monetize via read (data), write (tokenize/update) and transaction (settlement) layers.  
> * [**Credible neutrality**](https://assets.ctfassets.net/qtbqvna1l0yq/4hoFcrKkUm9Z5HiEX16g9J/e8a12f87497476841ff0d3defba944b8/Multicoin_Capital%C3%A2__s_Investment_Thesis.pdf): neutral base layers attract more issuers, liquidity and builders because financial firms won't build on a competitor's platform.  
> * [**Messaging is a moat, not a business**](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1FgcRLmPyyvExFYqBfBKKXsHVas12h4LtWfULzM5B5Go/edit?tab=t.0): messaging apps sell for very high prices (WhatsApp, Slack) because the acquirer uses them to protect a larger, profitable core, yet standalone messaging struggles to monetize: switching costs are near zero, free alternatives cap what users will pay, and premium tiers reach only a small share of users. Value ranks as a feature inside a high-LTV ecosystem first, a transactional superapp that takes a cut of commerce second (WeChat, LINE), and a standalone ad-supported company last (Snap). For a high-engagement product, ask whether it can tax the activity it enables or whether its value is mainly strategic to an acquirer.   
> * [**Feature vs. product**](https://x.com/a_vaunt): privacy has turned out to be a feature rather than something people pay for on its own, and most crypto AI agents traded like memecoins rather than useful products.

### **Market Timing**

> * **Capital environment**: is capital flowing into the space or leaving it, and what does that mean for entry prices?  
> * **Bear-market test**: does the thesis survive falling prices and scarce funding?  
> * **TAM trajectory**: is the addressable market growing, flat or shrinking?  
> * **Stage risk has [shifted](https://x.com/MikeIppolito_/status/1991174009367466245)**: crypto capital is overweight seed/Series A, a strategy that worked when tokens could pump without real value. Early stage is now riskier (must create value, more competition); the opportunity is backing scale-up winners that compound their lead.  
> * **Proven PMF [verticals](https://www.decentralised.co/p/non-consensus-and-right)**: stablecoins, prediction markets and perp exchanges have durable product-market fit; regulatory tailwinds (e.g. GENIUS Act) reduce the risk premium; exits now include M\&A, IPOs and token buybacks, not just TGEs. Seed investors need sector depth and 5-10 year holds.   
> * [**Vintage risk**](https://carta.com/data/2021-venture-funds-dpi/): returns are heavily tied to vintage year. Avoid deploying too fast in exuberant markets; diversify across time.   
> * **Interest vs. opportunity**: opportunity is lowest when interest in a theme peaks and highest after interest has faded to nothing. Rising attention is a signal that the best entry has passed; indifference is where the asymmetric setups are.

### **Macro Backdrop**

> * [**Monetary regime**](https://wtfhappenedin1971.com/): since the end of Bretton Woods in August 1971, debt has accumulated steadily, wages have lagged productivity and wealth has concentrated. Long-run monetary expansion favors scarce assets (gold, Bitcoin), productive assets that generate cash flow (equities, revenue-earning protocols) and hard assets (real estate, commodities).  
> * **Real vs. nominal gains**: use this backdrop to separate real value creation from returns that only reflect monetary inflation.  
> * **Alternative macro indicators**: build macro and sentiment signals from everyday datasets and look for correlations between data that seems unrelated. Established examples: the Big Mac Index, which compares burger prices across countries to judge whether currencies are over- or undervalued; Deutsche Bank's Oasis Index, the price of five beers and two packs of cigarettes across 69 cities as a cost-of-living gauge; and the lipstick, men's underwear, cardboard box, champagne and RV indices, which track small treats, deferred purchases, shipping volume and big-ticket spending. The positivity of the songs people stream on Spotify rises with same-week stock returns and predicts a reversal the following week, along with higher fund flows and volatility, across countries. Combine several into a composite rather than trading any single one. Candidates to test: Spotify valence, Google Trends for words like "recession" or "layoffs", and app store rankings for crypto and brokerage apps.  
> * **AI displacement [scenario](https://www.citriniresearch.com/p/2028gic)**: a thought exercise in which AI lifts margins and nominal GDP while displacing white-collar work, compressing labor's share and spreading stress from software into private credit and prime mortgages. Stress-test whether a portfolio assumes human intelligence stays scarce, software ARR stays recurring, and private credit/prime mortgages stay safe.   
> * [**Compute demand**](https://evjang.com/2026/02/04/rocks.html): reasoning models and agentic "automated research" workflows point to enormous, sustained demand for inference compute. Industry can't get enough [electricity, GPUs and fiber](https://x.com/Austin_Federa/status/2011758302410260543), and rack densities heading toward \~1 MW are forcing a full-stack shift to [800V DC power and liquid cooling](https://cruciblecapital.substack.com/p/building-a-datacenter-part-ii), with Nvidia's reference designs as the de facto standard and US datacenter demand forecast well over 100 GW by 2030\.

## **Verticals**

### **Distributed Networks**

Writing off distributed networks as a sector is like saying crypto isn't a coordination technology, and that what Bitcoin proved can't be applied to other real-world systems. The blockchain is an implementation detail that enables better coordination. To win users, a network has to reach parity with incumbents on both cost and performance, and those advantages have to be structural, coming from decentralization or distribution. A cost advantage then funds a product advantage. Decentralization for the sake of sovereignty is less important than creating net-better products.

If you had to summarize the issue with distributed networks and crypto incentives, it would be a conversion rate from cost-of-financing-per-node, whether that is a cost of equity or a cost of debt. The more expensive the hardware gets, the higher the payout needed to justify the higher cost threshold. This pushed projects toward lower-quality hardware to improve their metrics, since they needed to develop capital markets to justify expansion. Lower-quality hardware produces lower data fidelity, and without unique data you can't build a superior network.

Early entrants paid rewards for uptime and availability that weren't tied to usage, which built supply without demand. That can work in some cases, but the stronger approaches are usage-based rewards, where tokens are emitted only for valuable work, or InfraFi, where depositors finance the network and earn a strong APY from its revenue. Either way, supply leads and demand follows. Supply ramps fast on incentives and peaks early, then falls back as rewards thin out, while demand grows slowly and steadily until it crosses above supply. Strong early supply traction is key. The time between the supply peak and the crossover is the "all supply, no demand" phase, and that is when to start scaling demand. Once demand builds, the network becomes supply-constrained, and shifts in attention stall supply growth.

6th Man Ventures uses [four evaluation criteria](https://6thman.ventures/writing/depin-evaluation-framework.html): granular geographic importance, since the more granular the coverage, the harder it is to replicate; overlap between the supply and demand sides, since operators who use the product need fewer incentives and stay online; competing on performance, not just cost; and demand that scales nonlinearly with supply. Founder quality and regulatory hurdles sit on top of those. The best distributed network tokens show proven user demand driven by cost and speed advantages, multi-year records of more than 100% year-over-year revenue growth, end markets large enough to compound revenue for decades, growth through several crypto cycles, an improving regulatory environment, and prices at single-digit multiples of 2-3 year forward profits.

Storage is going through the same transition L1s did, from low-throughput (cold) to high-throughput (hot). Earlier decentralized storage networks work for archiving but not for live workloads. Newer designs target sub-second reads with onchain access rules and dynamic pricing, aimed at video streaming and AI training data that has to stay immediately accessible. 

### **Data**

AI models are running short of fresh, high-quality data, and the scarce inputs are now private and behavioral: company operating records, codebases, coding-agent traces, first-person video for robotics, and conversational audio. Money flows from buyers (frontier labs, big tech and robotics companies) through data labs, brokers and contributor networks back to the people and businesses that own the data.

Contributor networks are the distributed layer of this market, paying people for bandwidth, app exports, voice and video, sometimes in tokens, so the same supply-before-demand dynamics apply, and the rewards that matter are the ones tied to data buyers actually purchase. Across the market, look for recurring access rather than one-off sales, since ongoing licenses and multi-year exclusives already price higher, along with clean rights and consent, in-house extraction and de-identification, and real buyer contracts rather than contributor counts.

### **Unique Yield-Generating RWAs**

The thesis is to bring real-world assets onchain in a form where the blockchain is the first place they can be traded or borrowed against, rather than wrapping assets that already trade elsewhere. The value comes from new liquidity: holders can use the asset as DeFi collateral, and borrowers reach lenders they couldn't reach before. Tokens alone are a poor way to fund these assets. They are good at [incentivizing participation](https://cruciblecapital.substack.com/p/making-sense-of-depin-nonsense-part) and covering OpEx but poor at funding large upfront CapEx, and volatile token value against fiat-denominated hardware costs raises risk premiums, so use CAPM (cost of equity) and WACC as the hurdle rate against a project's IRR.

The main candidates are InfraFi, aviation leasing, REITs, HELOCs, auto loans, income funds, commodities and satellites. [High-value or regulated asset classes](https://www.lightshift.xyz/blog/asset-operating-system) may also justify dedicated chains with asset-specific privacy, compliance and transfer rules.

Two conditions drive the yield. Tokenized treasuries and yieldcoins track the Fed funds rate, so if rates return to near zero, as from 2008 to 2015 and 2020 to 2022, that yield disappears and demand for these products goes with it. Yield beyond treasuries needs real borrowers, and the AI and energy capex cycle currently supplies them, so these yields depend on that build-out continuing. If capex slows, borrower demand and yields fall.

### **Exchanges for net-new assets**

Exchanges act as the next iteration of financial institutions, bridging the previous financial system and the new one. The opportunity is in exchanges for assets that had no market before, rather than new venues for assets that already trade. Trading is already where crypto makes the most money, and the race between L1s is ultimately a race to build the global exchange.

Look for assets with real hedging demand from both producers and consumers, a credible price index or oracle, and a venue that can list new markets permissionlessly. The main risks are thin liquidity and pricing that relies on oracles and funding rates rather than arbitrage against the underlying, which is still unproven when the underlying market is closed.

### **Internet Labor Marketplaces**

[Internet labor markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vsg5-oAJvkw) are contributor-owned marketplaces that price, settle (in native tokens) and verify units of work. Tokens remove cold-start risk and enable micro-payouts, verification mixes cryptography with staking and slashing, and earning tokens is a main onboarding path into crypto.

## **The Fund**

### **Strategy and edge**

> * [**First fund**](https://x.com/hosseeb/status/2026747999939080217): put your reputation on the line and raise from everyone you know before moving upstream to family offices, funds of funds and institutions.   
> * [**Edge**](https://x.com/hosseeb/status/2026747999939080217): find a niche you're better at than anyone, however small; do the dirty work others avoid; optimize like a startup with tight feedback loops (track misses, study the biggest deals); have a concrete, stated ambition. Niches that LPs and consensus VC consider uninvestable have produced [very large firms](https://x.com/Melt_Dem/status/2014773902849835429).  
> * [**Brand**](https://x.com/hosseeb/status/2026747999939080217): early on, collect logos with small checks in hot deals and use them to get into the next deal; own your distribution by building personal audiences across the team; as you grow, cultivate power and a platform founders value.   
> * [**Value chain**](https://x.com/hosseeb/status/2026747999939080217): Sourcing → Selection → Winning → Supporting. Winning is the most important step; support feeds referrals back into sourcing and winning.   
> * [**Firms to study as capital compounders**](https://x.com/Melt_Dem/status/1996999716588945881): Thrive, Magnetar, Ribbit, Multicoin, Founders Fund, Hummingbird; especially firms innovating on structure, strategy, sector focus, and playing across the capital stack (e.g. credit sleeves).   
> * [**Permanent capital**](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-build-forever-money-%C3%A1lvaro-gorn%C3%A9s-dtb4f/): the binding constraint for a manager is redemptions and the fund calendar, not finding alpha. There are three routes to capital that can't leave: insurance float (Berkshire, Apollo with Athene, KKR with Global Atlantic), an operating compounder that recycles cash flows into acquisitions (Constellation Software), or a listed vehicle. Structure has to match strategy: Ackman's SPAC failed partly because exit-driven activist investing doesn't suit forever capital. Without it, fee income also blunts the incentive to generate alpha as AUM grows (the lifestyle business trap).  
> * [**Fixed-life fund distortions**](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-build-forever-money-%C3%A1lvaro-gorn%C3%A9s-dtb4f/): a 10-year LP fund forces deployment into expensive markets late in the investment period and sales on the fund's schedule rather than at peak value; continuation funds, where a manager sells an asset to its own next fund, are the industry's workaround.   
> * [**Holdco plus chain**](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-build-forever-money-%C3%A1lvaro-gorn%C3%A9s-dtb4f/): a holding company that buys cash-flowing businesses (payments, telecom, energy retail) and settles their activity on its own chain would earn both operating cash flows and protocol fees, with returns that grow the longer it holds. The author presents this as speculative and is building it at Inversion Capital. 

### **Value-add and the VC/founder relationship**

> * [**Survivors**](https://x.com/sjdedic/status/2042600374830248245): most crypto VCs won't survive the next few years. The ones that do get in the trenches with founders, have conviction that outlasts TGEs and bear markets, and help founders win attention/distribution, which becomes scarce when AI makes building cheap. Expect consolidation to 20-30 serious funds.  
> * [**Skeptical take on "value-add"**](https://x.com/arian_ghashghai/status/2027035719458246896): a VC's job is sourcing, picking and support; support rarely changes a company's trajectory. Most support time goes to the weakest companies, which drive little of the return; the main value is capital and acceleration for teams already executing.   
> * [**Scaling preferential attachment**](https://x.com/david__booth/status/1981377270792253847): traditional VC support is high-touch and doesn't scale; the opportunity is building networks/platforms that help portfolio companies keep attracting resources.   
> * [**Be honest, not "nice"**](https://x.com/hosseeb/status/1983248613728981284): good VCs are high-integrity and tell founders hard truths; "nice" VCs avoid conflict, rarely win deals and aren't there when things get tough. Founders sometimes need a [pitbull](https://x.com/yrechtman/status/1982999387069723088) more than a friendly face, and VCs who act in bad faith should face consequences.   
> * [**The real hard parts**](https://x.com/DAnconia_Crypto/status/1996771727364575625): the debates on podcasts (concentration vs. diversification, idea vs. founder, value-add vs. hands-off) are less hard in practice than keeping friends' trust as LPs, boundaries with portfolio execs, and loyalty vs. ambition. 

### **Deal structures**

> * [**Colosseum STAMP**](https://blog.colosseum.com/introducing-the-colosseum-stamp/): a contract bridging private investment into a MetaDAO ICO, giving investors an enforceable claim on future tokens and making the token the sole economic unit. Replaces the SAFE \+ token-warrant combo; investor reserve capped at 20% of supply with a 24-month linear unlock; milestone-based team allocation of 10-40%.

### **Operations (liquid positions)**

> * **Every crypto VC runs a liquid book once tokens vest**: plan custody, trading, hedging and exit policy for unlocked tokens from day one, even in a venture vehicle.  
> * Expect 6-12 months to [set up](https://medium.com/@L1D/how-to-launch-a-crypto-hedge-fund-2ca6a7173606) a fund, with meaningful pre-launch expenses. Cayman master-feeder is common; legally segregate share classes and side pockets.   
> * Manage counterparty and custody [risk](https://medium.com/@L1D/how-to-launch-a-crypto-hedge-fund-2ca6a7173606) with exposure limits, tri-party settlement and vetted custodians; keep at least two banking partners and diversified stablecoin holdings; actively supervise service providers and keep shadow books.   
> * [**Strategy map**](https://medium.com/@L1D/how-to-launch-a-crypto-hedge-fund-2ca6a7173606): L1D splits fund strategies into two styles. Fundamental covers venture capital (equity, debt; 10+ year liquidity), liquid ventures (SAFTs, staking, long-biased spot; about 2 years) and long/short (event-driven, multi-strategy; quarterly). Trading covers directional (systematic, discretionary) and market-neutral (market making, arbitrage, lending, DeFi), with weekly or monthly liquidity. Use it to place a fund's strategy and match LP liquidity terms to how long the assets take to realize.   
> * [**Markets**](https://docs.google.com/document/d/15c3rN15rkXldY8Te3GDG4NG7noaaoikydOoZQlElwXw/edit?tab=t.0)

### **Portfolio construction and returns**

> * [**Sizing discipline**](https://onchainwizard.substack.com/p/issue-4-intro-to-portfolio-management): set rules for stablecoin %, max position size and take-profit/stop-loss in advance; scale diversification with account size; take profits rather than marrying bags.   
> * [**LP expectations**](https://carta.com/data/2021-venture-funds-dpi/): hitting 3-5x net generally requires a unicorn-scale exit or two; small early acquisitions won't move the needle. 

### **Operator skills**

> * [**Cold outreach**](https://x.com/adriannalakatos/status/2042490448766525532): specific subject line, a hook instead of pleasantries, five lines max (hook, why it matters to them, ask, low-effort CTA, sign-off), an easy-yes ask, and follow-ups that carry news (e.g. traction) rather than bumps.   
> * [**Storytelling**](https://x.com/shotguncaio/status/2003867438685749259): open in the action, use contrast and concrete detail, hook in the first line, cut filler, end with impact, sell the transformation rather than the features.   
> * [**Annual review**](https://x.com/kepano/status/1602773345627348993): answer the same 40 questions at the end of every year (what you did for the first time, biggest achievement and failure, where most of the money went, what you'd do more and less of, the most valuable lesson) so patterns across years become visible.
